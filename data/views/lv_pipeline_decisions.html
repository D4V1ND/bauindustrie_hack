LV Pipeline

Decisions, prompts and test results · steps 0 to 4

What it does. It reads a tender's bill of quantities (the LV, Leistungsverzeichnis), links identical line items, sorts them into trades and item types, looks up prices in the company's own past projects, and gives every item a risk score.

Two of the five steps are plain code. Claude is asked in four places and never sees a price. A Voyage embedding model helps the search find other words for the same thing.

Part 1

The whole path

Two flows share the first two steps. One fills the price library, the other prices a new LV.

Where the AI is asked

JobModelEffortCalls
Propose the item type listClaude Opus 5.5medium1 per LV
Give each item its type and a reasonClaude Sonnet 5.5high1 per 25 items
Judge whether a past item is the same thingClaude Sonnet 5.5xhigh1 per item type, at most 15 items
Turn item texts into vectors for the search by meaningVoyage AI voyage-4noneonce per text, then saved
Say whether an item's description is complete enough to priceClaude Sonnet 5.5xhigh1 per 25 items
Read a construction plan, only when one is givenOpenAI vision model (OPENAI_MODEL)default3 readings per plan, plus up to 3 for matching

Effort is how long the model thinks before it answers. All three run through langchain-anthropic, with answers in a fixed shape that code validates. At effort high the judge answered without thinking at all, so it now runs at xhigh. The vectors come through the voyageai package; without a Voyage key the search uses words only.

Rules that hold in every step

RuleWhat it means
Own data onlyPrices come from the company's past projects. No internet prices.
The AI states no priceIt reads item texts and answers with a type or a verdict. It is never sent a price, and code does all arithmetic.
Every AI decision keeps its reasonThe reason is saved next to the decision in the result file, so the app can answer "why?" without guessing afterwards. The judge also keeps every verdict, and each of its calls is logged with the question it read and its summarised thinking.
The file's prices stay untouchedA looked-up price is saved as a prediction beside the item. It is never written into the item's own unit price.

The AI reads texts. Code handles every number.

Part 2 · Step 0

Read the file

Every LV file becomes the same table: one row per line item.

Code This whole step is deterministic. No AI is involved.

PartWhat we decidedWho

How it tested
  • Item counts match the files: P1 159, P2 248, P4 296, P5 50.
  • P4 has 24 optional items. The other three have none.
  • For P1 and P2, the item totals add up to the sums the priced files state themselves.
  • The priced files hold more records than the unpriced ones: 38 in P1 and 1 in P2. They are change orders and are kept.
  • P3 from its PDFs: all 274 priced items found their text in both prints, with matching quantity and unit. 168 have a long text. The build session checked the other 106 and found none in the PDF either: 89 are "as before" items, 16 hourly rates, 1 a fitting.

Part 3 · Step 1

Link identical items

Items that are word for word the same get the same label.

Code This whole step is deterministic. No AI is involved.

PartWhat we decidedWho
What counts as identicalThe same short text, long text and unit.Code
What is ignoredUpper and lower case, extra spaces, line breaks, a hyphen from a line break ("We- ge" reads as "Wege"), and the different spellings the anonymiser uses for one hidden word.Code
What is never ignoredAnything with meaning. DN 150 and DN 200 get different labels, and so does the same text in metres and in pieces.Code
The labelA short code calculated from the three cleaned texts, saved on the item as group.Code
What it is used forAdding up repeats inside one LV, copying a price when a past item is identical, and asking the AI only once per set of identical items.Code
How it tested
  • Inside one LV: P2 has 27 sets of identical items covering 55 items. P4 has 7 sets covering 16. P5 has none.
  • One LV in two file formats: 160 of P2's 248 items get the same label in its .X83 and its .D86. The other 88 are worded differently in the two files: the anonymiser hid different words in each, and GAEB 90 cuts short texts at 70 characters.
  • Across projects: 1 of P4's 296 items is identical to an item in the price library, and none of P5's 50.
  • 20 automated tests cover the label and the search that step 3 builds on.

Exact matching links repeats inside an LV. Across projects it almost never fires.

Part 4 · Step 2

Sort into trades and item types

A 296-item LV becomes about 70 kinds of work, each with its quantities. For the new LV only.

AICode The AI proposes and assigns. Code checks every answer and adds up.

PartWhat we decidedWho
Three levelsTrade, then item type, then the group of identical items from step 1. Trades come from a fixed list of 17 stand-in names.Decision
Propose the item type listOne call reads every item of the LV and names its kinds of work, each with one trade and a one-line description. At most one type per four items, never fewer than 20 allowed and never more than 100.AIOpus, medium
Clean the listRemoves double names, types named after a surcharge, catch-all types and anything over the cap.Code
Assign each item25 items per call. Each item gets one type from the list, or "Sonstiges" (other) if none fits, plus one sentence saying why.AISonnet, high
Check the answersA type outside the list is asked again once, then becomes "Sonstiges". Identical items are asked once and share the answer. The reason never changes the outcome.Code
The overviewQuantities added up per trade, item type and unit, never across units. Only normal items are summed.Code
Past projectsGet no trades or item types. Sorting is for the overview of the new LV, not for prices.Decision

The reading was done by the assistant, item by item: both LVs on the Haiku run, and P5 on one Sonnet run. It is not an estimator's judgement, and the current run has not been read that way. Before anything was built, a first test on P1 and P5 found 1 clear mistake in 209 items.

Weak spots
  • The type list changes from run to run. Three runs of P4 proposed 73, 74 and 72 types. The middle one had no type for hourly rates, so all 25 of them went to "Sonstiges".
  • Surcharges are added into the quantity sums. A surcharge takes the type of the item it belongs to, and P4 has about 91 surcharge items. Metres of trench and metres of a surcharge on that trench are added together.
  • The trade list is a stand-in. In the Haiku run, 10 items had a sensible type but no fitting trade.
  • 30 automated tests, all with a stand-in for the AI.
Part 5 · Step 3

Look up a price

A lookup, not an estimate. An item gets a price only when the library holds the same item.

CodeAI Code finds and calculates. The AI only judges whether two texts describe the same thing.

OutcomeWhenIn the total?
copiedA past item is word for word identical.Yes
similarThe judge calls at least one past item the same thing.Yes
adjustedOne past item is the same thing in a smaller size and one in a larger size. Code interpolates between the two prices and saves the calculation.Yes
referenceOnly past items of the same kind were found. They are shown with their prices, and no price is proposed.No
noneNothing close.No

How one item is handled

PartWhat we decidedWho
CopyA past item with the same label from step 1 gives its price. No AI is asked.Code
SearchAcross the whole library: same unit, surcharges only against surcharges. Two rankings: by words (shared words, rare ones weigh more) and by meaning (Voyage vectors of the short text and the first 500 characters of the long text). The best 20 of each are merged by their places (reciprocal rank fusion), and the top 5 go to the judge. An "as before" item is compared together with the text it refers to.Codevectors by Voyage
JudgeReads the new item and its candidates, texts only. One verdict per candidate: same, size only, same kind only, or different. In doubt, the stricter one.AISonnet, xhigh
Packing the questionsIdentical items are judged once. Items of one item type travel in one call, at most 15, with the type named as a hint.Code
Check the answersEach item must come back with exactly its own candidates. Otherwise it is asked again on its own.Code
Price and totalLowest, highest, median, mean and count of the past prices, with every source. The total is median × quantity over normal items that were copied, similar or adjusted.Code
Saved for "why?"Per item: the 3 best candidates, best first, each with its verdict, what differs, and one sentence saying why it is the match or why it is not, plus its short text and price. The judge still reads 5, because a shortlist of 3 would have lost one of P5's five prices; the judge's sentence; for a copy, the item that was actually judged. Per judge call, in a log beside the result file: the question as sent, the judge's summarised thinking and its answer. The format for the frontend is in docs/result-format.md.Decision

Search by meaning: what it changed

Meaning search gave every P5 item at least one candidate (by words alone, 43 of 50). P5's 9 prices at high included four doubtful ones: a "Transporter" the judge itself called ambiguous, a 12 t truck at P3's token price of 1.06 per hour, a foreman rate from a plant engineering job, and a joint tape. At xhigh exactly those four became references.

One item, end to end
  • P4's gate valve "Absperrschieber … PN16 DN80, Handbetätigung" was 8th by words against P1's "Absperrschieber DN 80, PN 16 … mit Handrad". By meaning it was 1st (0.835), so it made the shortlist.
  • A single judge call said same: 258.48 per piece, where the words-only run had no price. In the full P4 run the judge said same kind only: "no material or coating stated". Both are defensible, and together they show that the judge, not the search, now decides most outcomes.
  • Meaning blurs numbers: P5's "Pass- und Gelenkstück DN 800" comes closest to a "Bogen DN 80". A score cut-off cannot separate right from wrong, so the judge reads every candidate.

Before the build: 720 hidden prices
  • A prototype hid the prices of one past project at a time and predicted them from the others, using P1, P2 and P3.
  • Loose rule: priced 17 to 30% of items, typically 80 to 120% off. Only 11 to 12% landed within 25% of the real price.
  • Strict rule: priced 5 to 9 of the 720 items, depending on the judge. The judge with the estimator's own rules priced 5, and none was off by more than 25%.
  • These figures come from the test session and were not re-run for this page. They are why step 3 is strict.
Weak spots
  • The judge is not fully repeatable. P5's 4 cm asphalt layer was priced in two of five runs. When it was, the verdict said "same" while the judge's own note said the mix differs (AC 8 against AC 11). With the new search it flipped again on P4: the DN 80 gate valve and three identical "clear the site" lump sums (7,500.00 each) were same in one run and same kind only in the next. At effort high it answered without thinking; at xhigh it thinks and is stricter.
  • Lump sums travel badly. P5's site setup, for a stream culvert, takes its 5,700.00 from a plant engineering job. On P4, three identical "clear the site" lump sums at 7,500.00 each from P3 make up 22,500.00 of the 22,532.74 total.
  • P3's bid holds near-zero prices. 56 of its 274 unit prices are below 2, such as 0.05 per m2 for laying geotextile. Through the search by meaning two of them reached a price: P4's construction fence at 0.10 per m, and, at effort high, P5's 12 t truck at 1.06 per hour. No rule handles them yet.
  • No accuracy check for the built version. The command that hides one past project and predicts it is not built.
  • The step 3 tests use stand-ins for the judge and for Voyage; 172 automated tests in all.

Low coverage is accepted. A wrong price is not.

Part 6 · Step 4

Score the risk

One risk score per item, from how sure the price is and how complete the description is. One score for the LV, where expensive items count more.

CodeAI Code scores the price risk and does the weighting. The AI only reads each item's text.

PartWhat we decidedWho
Price riskFrom the step 3 outcome: copied 5, similar 25, adjusted 45, reference 75, none 100. A similar price whose past prices lie more than 10% apart gets 40.Code
Description riskDoes the text state what an estimator needs: kind, material, size, class, scope, and for hourly rates the worker or machine? Complete 10, gaps 50, vague 90. The model names up to 3 missing things and gives one sentence. 25 items per call.AISonnet, xhigh
The item's score0.6 × price risk + 0.4 × description risk. A place on a scale from 0 to 100, not a probability.Code
How much an item countsBy its value: the found price × quantity. Without a price: the middle price of its related past items × quantity. With nothing: the value of a typical item of this LV. The value is a weight and is never shown as a price.Code
The LV's scoreThe items' scores weighted by value, over the normal items. Saved with it: how many values rest on a price, on related items, or on an assumption.Code
The AI does not rate importanceAsking the model how big an item is would be a price guess, which the rules forbid.Decision

Examples from P5: "Asphalt schneiden" scores 19 (same past item found, text complete). "Transporter" scores 65 (only related items; vehicle size and operator not stated). "Pass- und Gelenkstück DN 800" scores 80 (nothing found; length or angle not stated).

Weak spots
  • The weights are rough. The three items that move P5's score most are all weighted by related items' prices, not by a found price:
    • Rohrgrabenaushub, bis 2m, seitlich lagern, wieder einbauen: score 49, value about 39,388 (from related items).
    • Rohrgrabenaushub, Abfuhr auf AG-Zwischenlager: score 49, value about 27,300 (from related items).
    • Rohrauflager und -einbettung, Feinkies 0/32 mm, bis DN 900 m: score 49, value about 14,605 (from related items).
  • Values inherit bad past prices, near-zero ones included.
  • Where step 3 finds little, most values are assumed. On P4, 178 of 296 items had no candidate the judge accepted.
  • The scale is a first guess. All numbers are starting values to tune with an estimator.
  • The description check waits for the judge. It needs only the texts and the item types, so it could run beside the judge and save about 2 minutes on P4.
  • 5 automated tests, with a stand-in for the AI.

A score says where to look first. The estimator decides.

Bottom line

Reading, linking and sorting work on any LV today. The price lookup is honest but thin: with three past projects and the search by meaning, the judge at xhigh prices 5 of P5's 50 items and shows the nearest past items for 33 more. Step 4 puts P5's risk at 51 of 100 and P4's at 52. More past LVs of the same kind of work will change that more than any prompt.
