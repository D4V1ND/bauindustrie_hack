The 9 candidates and where each came from

A candidate is a past priced item that might be the same thing as the new item. For each new item, step 3 searches all 720 library items and keeps a short list: the 5 best by words and the 5 best by meaning. The butterfly valve P2/09.01.0017 was on both lists, so 5 + 5 − 1 = 9 candidates. The judge then checks every candidate against the new item. Only a same or size only verdict can lead to a price; here 1 of the 9 did.

Both scores compare the same texts, short text plus long text; they differ in how. Word score: how many words the two texts share, with rare words counting more (whole long text). Meaning score: how close the two texts are in meaning, from Voyage vectors (first 500 characters of the long text). It also matches different words for the same thing, such as "Handbetätigung" and "Handrad".

For the estimator

  • Check the specification. P1 is a plant engineering project; its valve is "as specified" without the details P4 asks for (soft-sealing, epoxy coating, drinking water). The judge found nothing that contradicts, but it could not confirm those details either.
  • One item, one run. This page shows how the matching works. Whether vector search adds more correct prices than wrong ones across whole LVs is measured by the hidden-project test, which has not run yet.
  • Scores only choose candidates. Neither score sets a price. Only a same or size only verdict from the judge leads to a price, and the price is always a past price or an interpolation between two.

Result format: what the frontend gets

lv price writes two JSON files for each new LV. The first holds every line item with its step 2 and step 3 results; the second logs every judge call, so a "why?" view can show what the judge read and thought. Both examples below are real, from the latest P5 run (judge at effort xhigh); only the judge log entry is cut to one item.

The exact shapes are JSON Schemas generated from the code: docs/result.schema.json and docs/judge-log.schema.json, explained in docs/result-format.md.

The structure

P5.json                          the LV, one file per new LV
├─ project, name, date, source_file, priced
├─ item_types[]                  {name, trade, description}                      step 2
├─ summary                       {methods: {<method>: {items, total}}, total}    step 3
└─ items[]                       one per line item, in LV order
   ├─ id, oz, section[], short_text, long_text, quantity, unit, kind, group
   ├─ item_type, trade, item_type_reason                                         step 2
   └─ prediction                                                                 step 3
      ├─ method                  copied | similar | adjusted | reference | none
      ├─ median, min, max, mean, count     the price; only for copied, similar, adjusted
      ├─ sources[]               {id, short_text, unit_price, verdict, difference, why}   what the price rests on
      ├─ judged[]                same shape: the 3 best candidates, best first, each with a why
      ├─ reason                  the judge's sentence for this item
      ├─ rule, calculation, result         only for adjusted
      └─ judged_item             on a copy: the identical item that was judged

P5_judge.json                    one entry per judge call (up to 15 items of one item type)
└─ [ ]
   ├─ call                       1, 2, 3 … in the order the calls were made
   ├─ items[]                    ids of the items this call asked about
   ├─ question                   exactly what the judge read, texts only, no prices
   ├─ thinking                   the judge's summarised thinking ("" when it did not think)
   └─ answer.items[]             {id, judged[{cand, verdict, dimension, cand_size, target_size, difference, why}], reason}
